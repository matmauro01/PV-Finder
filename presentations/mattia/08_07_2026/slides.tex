\begin{frame}{Motivation}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.52\linewidth}
            \textbf{Primary-vertex finding} reconstructs the proton--proton
            interaction points along the beam axis from charged-particle tracks.
            It underlies essentially every LHC measurement.

            \vspace{0.55em}
            \textbf{PV-Finder} is a neural network that maps tracks directly to a
            1-D histogram of vertex density, then extracts positions by peak finding.

            \vspace{0.55em}
            \textbf{Key advantage:} the current default, the Adaptive Multi-Vertex
            Finder (\textbf{AMVF}), is iterative: its runtime grows with the number of
            vertices (${\sim}200$\,ms/event at $\mu{=}90$). PV-Finder evaluates a
            fixed-size map, so its inference is \textbf{flat with pileup}, critical for
            Run 3 ($\langle\mu\rangle\!\approx\!60$) and HL-LHC ($\langle\mu\rangle\!\approx\!200$).
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.48\linewidth}
            \begin{figure}
                \centering
                \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/timing_1.png}
            \end{figure}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

    \end{tikzpicture}}\par}

    \vspace{0.7em}
    \begin{columns}[t]
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            \textbf{What goes in and what comes out}
            \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.25em}
                \item Input is the event's \textbf{track set}: $7$ features per track,
                    the impact parameters $d_0$ and $z_0$, their errors and covariance,
                    and the track's $z$ extent
                \item The \textbf{masked MLP} treats the tracks as a set, so the output
                    is invariant to their order and to how many there are
                \item The \textbf{1-D U-Net} turns the latent map into a vertex-density
                    histogram: $12$ intervals of $40$\,mm, $12{,}000$ bins of
                    $0.04$\,mm over $|z| < 240$\,mm
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            \textbf{How it is trained}
            \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.25em}
                \item $3.55$M parameters, plain \textbf{MSE} against a target histogram
                    of Gaussian peaks, one per truth vertex
                \item The target peak \emph{width} encodes how well a vertex of that
                    multiplicity can be located, and is measured from data (Part III)
                \item An \textbf{MLP-only warm-up} precedes end-to-end training. This is
                    what removes the kernel-density estimate from the pipeline entirely
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.3em}
                \item \textbf{Scan} the $12{,}000$ bins left to right and collect every
                    contiguous run of bins above $\tau = 0.01$
                \item \textbf{Split conjoined peaks.} Once the density has passed a
                    maximum and starts rising again, the run closes and a new one opens
                    at that local minimum, so two peaks that overlap without returning
                    to zero are still separated
            \end{enumerate}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.35em}\setcounter{enumi}{2}
                \item \textbf{Quality cuts} on each closed run: at least $3$ bins wide,
                    integral $\geq 0.30$, maximum amplitude $\geq 0.03$
                \item \textbf{Position} from a local centroid about the maximum (next slide)
            \end{enumerate}
            \vspace{0.4em}

            \pvcap{Every parameter above is a post-inference knob. The split rule has
            \textbf{no noise tolerance}, which is what resolves close pairs and also
            what creates the surplus peaks seen in Part III.}
        \end{column}

    \begin{columns}[t]
        \begin{column}{0.52\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            When a run is split, what is left is only \textbf{half of a peak}. Averaging
            over the whole run then lands off center, pulled toward the side the split
            did not cut.
            \vspace{0.45em}

            So we average over the \textbf{maximum bin and three bins on each side},
            stopping at the edge of the run so we never pick up a neighbor's bins. We
            scanned that window from $2$ bins out to the whole run: three is best, and
            the curve is flat around it. It costs $0.34$ efficiency points and gives a
            $13\%$ better separation scale.

\begin{frame}{Resolution Metric: \(\sigma_{\text{vtx-vtx}}\)}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.38\linewidth}
            \footnotesize
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.45em}
                \item All \textbf{pairwise} separations $\Delta z = z_i - z_j$ between
                    reconstructed PVs in an event
                \item Pairs too close to resolve are \textbf{merged} by the peak finder,
                    so the histogram is suppressed near $\Delta z = 0$
                \item A \textbf{sigmoid} fit turns that suppression into a number
                \item $\sigma_{\text{vtx-vtx}}$ is the inflection half-width, the
                    \textbf{smallest resolvable separation}
            \end{itemize}

            locates a vertex of a given multiplicity ($8.5\times10^{6}$ matched pairs),
            and fit $\sigma(\ntrk) = A\,\ntrk^{-B} + C$.
            \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
                \item Each bin is fit with a Gaussian core on a flat background, so
                    wrong matches do not widen the answer
                \item On this sample $A{=}0.143$, $B{=}0.362$. \ntrk\ now includes
                    forward tracks, which say little about $z$, so peaks come out wider
                    at the same \ntrk
                \item The Run 3 widths are about twice as large. That gap is the ITk
                    $z_0$ improvement
            \end{itemize}
            \vspace{0.4em}
            {\scriptsize\textcolor{black!55}{Narrower targets are not free: a small gain
            in efficiency, but measurably more surplus peaks
            ($+0.018 \pm 0.003$ per peak, $6.5\sigma$).}}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\partdivider{IV}{Track-to-vertex association}{A graph network that completes the chain}

\begin{frame}{TTVA: What It Is and Why We Need It}
    \textbf{Vertex finding tells us \emph{where} the vertices are, but not \emph{which tracks belong to which}.}
    \vspace{0.5em}
    \begin{center}
        tracks \(\to\) PV-Finder histogram \(\to\) peak finding \(\to\) candidate vertices
        \(\to\) \textcolor{pennred}{\textbf{TTVA}} \(\to\) per-vertex track lists
    \end{center}
    \vspace{0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[t]
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \textbf{Why analyses need track lists:}
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
                \item Hard-scatter choice $=$ vertex with largest $\sum p_T^2$ of \emph{its} tracks
                \item Pile-up jet rejection, and any per-vertex quantity (mass, multiplicity, pointing)
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \textbf{Why we need it for a fair comparison:}
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
                \item AMVF gets track lists free from its fit; PV-Finder does not
                \item With TTVA both chains are judged by the \emph{same} track-based purity criteria
                \item Design choice: \textbf{train on MC truth vertices}, \textbf{deploy on PV-Finder peaks}
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{TTVA: The Event as a Graph}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.52\linewidth}
            \small
            Each event is a \textbf{bipartite graph} with two node types:
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
                \item \textcolor{mydarkblue}{\textbf{Track nodes}} (8 feat.): $d_0, z_0, \sigma_{d_0}, \sigma_{z_0}, \mathrm{cov}, \theta, \phi, p_T$
                \item \textcolor{myorange}{\textbf{Vertex nodes}} (2 feat.): $z$ position, peak height
                \item \textbf{Edges} = (track, vertex) pairs, each carrying 3 physics attributes:
                \[
                  \frac{z_0 - z_{PV}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{z_0}^2+\sigma_{PV}^2}},\quad
                  \frac{d_0}{\sigma_{d_0}},\quad |z_0 - z_{PV}|
                \]
                \item Label: edge $=1$ iff MC truth links the track to that vertex
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.46\linewidth}
            \centering
            \begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.85cm, y=0.85cm]

\begin{frame}{TTVA: A Small Graph Attention Network}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.55\linewidth}
            \begin{enumerate}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
                \item Embed track (8) and vertex (2) features into 32 dimensions
                \item \textbf{2 rounds of heterogeneous graph attention} (4 heads): each node updates from its neighbors, the \textbf{edge attributes steer the attention}
                \item[] {\small\(\Rightarrow\) a vertex that already attracts compatible tracks attracts the next one more strongly, context a per-pair cut cannot express}
                \item A 3-layer MLP on each (track, vertex) embedding \(\to\) \textbf{one score per edge}
            \end{enumerate}
            \vspace{0.2em}
            {\footnotesize\textbf{Size \& training:} \textbf{39{,}521 parameters} ($90\times$
            smaller than PV-Finder), $3.8$\,ms/event; BCE loss, Adam, trained on 180k PU200 events in 3.2\,h.}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.45\linewidth}
            \centering

            the classifier is nearly saturated, with ROC \textbf{AUC 0.998} on $45$M edges.}}

    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{TTVA: From Scores to Vertices}
    \small
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.56\linewidth}
            \textbf{Selection (MaxScore):} each track goes to its highest-scoring vertex,
            but only if that score clears a threshold \(t\); otherwise it stays unassigned.
            The model runs \textbf{once}, and \(t\) is a post-hoc knob (scores saturate near 1,
            so the useful range is \(t\geq0.9\)).
            \vspace{0.5em}

            \textbf{Two conventions that must always be named:}
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.25em}
                \item \textbf{Drop-empty}: vertices that end up with no track are
                    dropped rather than booked Fake. Clean-over-truth is unchanged and
                    higher thresholds become usable
                \item The purity cut is $\mathbf{70\%}$, and it is load-bearing: the
                    AMVF vertex-purity distribution has median $0.63$ and straddles it
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
    \begin{columns}[t]
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \textbf{What we have shown}
            \begin{enumerate}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
                \item \small An end-to-end trainable model with the kernel-density
                    estimate removed from training, validated on Run 2 and Run 3 data
                \item \small At low pileup it matches AMVF and \textbf{beats it in
                    resolution}, $0.42$ against $0.79$\,mm
                \item \small At \textbf{PU200}, on held-out events and a window that
                    favors neither algorithm, it beats AMVF by
                    \textbf{$+3.4 \pm 0.1$ efficiency points}, with
                    $0.60 \pm 0.09$ fewer surplus vertices per event and
                    $\sigma_{\text{vtx-vtx}} = 0.229$\,mm against $0.305$
                \item \small \textbf{TTVA completes the chain}, at
                    \textbf{94.0\,\%} of the association oracle against AMVF's
                    $42.5\,\%$, for ${\sim}11$\,ms/event end to end
            \end{enumerate}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \textbf{Four framing findings}
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.5em}
                \item \small Capacity is \textbf{not} the bottleneck. A $5\times$
                    larger model, and a sweep over learning rate, optimizer and loss,
                    all reach the same plateau
                \item \small Part of the residual inefficiency is \textbf{physical}:
                    ${\sim}17\%$ of adjacent PU200 vertices are closer than the
                    resolution
                \item \small \textbf{Post-hoc fake suppression is done}. None of 817
                    configurations beat the operating point we already run, so the rest
                    has to come from training
                \item \small The headroom is the \textbf{finder's}. Of the $27.2$
                    points between truth and the association oracle, $21.9$ are truth
                    vertices with no peak at all
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Next Steps}
    \begin{columns}[t]
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \textbf{Finder side, all of it needs a retrain}
            \begin{itemize}
                \setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
                \item \small \textbf{Replace the nearest-neighbor upsampling} in the
                    U-Net decoder. Surplus peaks pile up at the upsampling period, a
                    $35\%$ modulation that real peaks do not show
                \item \small \textbf{Make the target drawable}. We ask for peaks
                    narrower than one bin for $31.6\%$ of truth vertices, with
                    amplitudes up to $22\times$. Cap the width at one bin, or use a
                    finer grid near the peaks
                \item \small \textbf{An objectness head, or a fake-aware loss}. The
                    only idea here that attacks surplus peaks at the source
            \end{itemize}

                \item \small Re-run the inference benchmark on the current model under

        \begin{column}{0.48\linewidth}
            \textbf{Chain fakes are the finder's, not the associator's.} The difference
            between the $13.9\%$ junk rate and the $9.1\%$ oracle floor is a
            truth-convention artifact: a peak sitting on a real single-track interaction
            has truth \emph{tracks} but no eligible truth \emph{vertex}.
            \vspace{0.6em}

            {\footnotesize\textcolor{black!55}{Resampling the training vertex nodes to
            match the measured peak distributions was the obvious fix. It was tested and
            it does not work at equal training budget, see Part IV.}}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: The GBT Fake Classifier}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \small
            \textbf{Setup:} a gradient-boosted-tree classifier scores each peak real vs
            fake (real $=$ within $0.5$\,mm of a truth vertex), from \textbf{23 per-peak features}:
            \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
                \item track counts and quality in windows around the peak
                \item histogram shape (height, local integral, FWHM, curvature)
                \item neighborhood (distance and ratio to the nearest peak)
            \end{itemize}
            \vspace{0.4em}
            Trained on one PU200 run, evaluated on an \textbf{independent} run with no
            event overlap.
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.5\linewidth}
            \small
            \textbf{Ablation (AUC):} all 23 $=0.965$, histogram-only $=0.959$,
            track-only $=0.957$. The two groups are \textbf{largely redundant}; the
            single most important feature is the \textbf{local integral} within
            $\pm0.5$\,mm, importance $0.72$.
            \vspace{0.5em}

            \textbf{The gate does not transfer between models.} On the model it was
            trained for, threshold $0.3$ removed ${\sim}38\%$ of fakes at $0.9$ points
            of efficiency. On the current model the same threshold removes
            \textbf{$7.4\%$}. Discrimination survives (AUC $0.949$), but the
            \textbf{calibration} moved by a factor of five.
            \vspace{0.5em}

            {\footnotesize\textcolor{black!55}{The discriminating signal does live in the
            histogram, which is the argument for an in-model head that cleans the
            histogram at training time rather than a post-hoc gate.}}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: Removing the KDE Supervision}
    \vfill
    \centering
    \scriptsize
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}

    the U-Net can mask a degenerate one is what prevents the first-bin collapse,

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: Loss Re-weighting, a Negative Result}
    \begin{columns}[c]
        \begin{column}{0.55\linewidth}
            \small
            \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
                \item The target peak height rises steeply with \ntrk, so under MSE
                    \textbf{missing a tall peak costs $50\times$ more} than missing a
                    short one, and the misses live at low \ntrk
                \item Tested a weighted MSE that flattens the ratio to $8\times$, three
                    arms trained in parallel
                \item At \textbf{matched fake rate} the arms are
                    \textbf{indistinguishable}, and plain MSE has the best resolution
            \end{itemize}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.45\linewidth}
            \small
            \textbf{Verdict: keep plain MSE.}
            \vspace{0.5em}

            The apparent gain visible on the training monitor was an amplitude-scale
            artifact, not a change in the vertices found.
            \vspace{0.5em}

            {\footnotesize\textcolor{black!55}{This is why the remaining fake-suppression
            ideas are aimed at the \emph{target definition} and at an explicit
            objectness output, rather than at re-weighting the same target.}}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}
